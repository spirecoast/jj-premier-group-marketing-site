System architecture

What the JJ Premier Group system does for the business, how it is built, and what enabling voice actually changes.

Version 3. Rewritten after a full review of the Follow Up Boss and Sanity documentation. Prepared by Spire Coast LLC.

Business architecture

Before any of the technology, this is what the system is for. A two-agent team competing in Lakewood Ranch and Sarasota cannot outspend the portals on advertising and cannot out-staff a large brokerage on follow-up. What it can do is know the area better than anyone and never let a conversation go cold. The system is built to convert both of those into business.

The commercial argument in one paragraph

Portal leads are rented and expensive. Content leads are owned and compound. A calendar and a set of neighborhood pages cost time once and keep producing; a portal lead costs money every time. This system is a bet that a small team's local knowledge, published properly and followed up quickly, beats buying attention. Voice is in the plan because the follow-up half is where small teams usually lose.

Where voice actually sits

This is the section that changed most in this revision, and it is worth being exact, because the wrong assumption here would have shaped the whole build.

What enabling voice in Follow Up Boss gives you

Follow Up Boss records calls made through its own calling system and, within seconds of the call ending, produces a transcript and an AI summary with action items, which appear on the lead's timeline in both the web and mobile apps. The summary generates suggested tasks the agent can accept. The known constraints: it is a power-up that only an account owner can enable, recording and AI summaries are coupled and cannot be turned on separately, only calls longer than fifteen seconds and shorter than an hour are processed, transferred calls are not, and it is English only.

What it does not do

It does not write to the website. Speaking into Follow Up Boss will not create a listing or add an event to the calendar. The reason is specific rather than a matter of effort: the transcript and summary are product features, and the public API does not expose them. The calls endpoint accepts a note, an outcome, a duration and a recording URL, and the documentation carries an explicit warning that some call data is only visible inside the Follow Up Boss application. There is a webhook that fires when a call is created, so an outside system can learn that a call happened and go read what the API will give it, but the AI-written summary is not among the fields it can retrieve.

The correction, stated plainly

Voice in Follow Up Boss is call intelligence, not content entry. It belongs on the conversion side of the business loop, not the publishing side.

This is not a downgrade. Call intelligence is the more valuable of the two for a two-person team: the calendar can be typed in five minutes a week, whereas remembering what forty leads said on the phone cannot be done at all.

The value it does deliver

  1. The agents stop taking notes during callsWhich means they listen instead of typing, and the record is better than the one they would have written.
  2. Every lead has a memoryA lead who called in March and calls again in July arrives with their earlier conversation attached, next to the pages and listings they viewed on the site.
  3. Follow-up stops depending on disciplineSuggested tasks come out of the summary, so the next step exists whether or not anyone remembered to create it.
  4. The loop closesReading back a month of call summaries tells the agents what people in this market are actually asking. That is the input for the next round of neighborhood pages and calendar entries, and it is information no keyword tool will give them.
Two compliance items, and one is Florida-specific

Florida requires the consent of all parties to record a communication, unlike the many states where one party's consent is enough. Follow Up Boss offers a call recording disclosure feature that plays an announcement before connecting. Recording must not be switched on until the disclosure is configured and the wording has been reviewed. Confirm the approach with Vinnie and, given the exposure, with counsel rather than treating this as a settings question.

Separately, texting leads requires 10DLC registration, and that registration checks the website: the contact form must carry texting opt-in language and the privacy policy must state that collected phone numbers are not shared for marketing. Both are build tasks on this site, not Follow Up Boss settings, and the registration has approval lead time.

If spoken content entry is still wanted

It is buildable, but it is a separate small system on the Sanity side rather than anything to do with Follow Up Boss: audio captured in the browser, transcribed by a speech service, shaped into a schema-correct draft by one of Sanity's AI actions, and left as a draft for a human to publish. Dictation is batch work rather than live conversation, so it runs on the cheapest tier of any transcription service and the cost is immaterial at this volume. Sanity's AI actions are still marked experimental, so this is a phase three item, not a launch dependency. It is written up here so the option stays open, not because the calendar needs it.

Technical architecture

Design source of record

The visual system for this build is defined in the JJ Premier Group brand system at jj-premier-brand-system.vercel.app, alongside the website mockup published in the same place. Those documents govern color, typography, spacing, and component styling; this architecture document mirrors their tokens rather than defining any of its own, and the block at the top of this file is the single place where the values are set.

One practical note: that host refuses automated access, which means the tokens have to be transcribed by hand rather than read programmatically. It is a one-time transcription into the Tailwind theme and this file, so it does not affect the build, but it does mean the brand system and the code can drift if the brand system is later revised. Worth agreeing now that the brand system stays the master and any change there gets mirrored into the theme deliberately.

Content model

Five document types carry the site. Listings and events are the two that matter most, for different reasons: listings are what the business sells, and events are what brings people to the site when there is nothing to sell them.

listing      address, price, beds, baths, sqft, status, gallery,
             description, features, mlsNumber, neighborhood→, featured

event        title, slug, summary, startsAt, endsAt, allDay,
             venue→, category, ticketUrl, priceNote, image, source

venue        name, slug, address, geo, neighborhood→, website, image

neighborhood name, slug, hero, overview, highlights, listings→, events→

post         title, slug, cover, body, date, author→
teamMember   name, headshot, title, licenseNumber, bio, phone, email

Venues are their own type rather than a text field, because the same venues recur and a venue page is a useful local page in its own right. Events reference a neighborhood, which is what lets a neighborhood page show what is happening there this month. That single reference is most of the calendar's value: a neighborhood page with a live events list is a substantially better page than one made of adjectives, and it is the page that will rank.

One constraint to design around

Sanity's document count includes drafts and internal records, and the free tier caps it. A calendar accumulating events indefinitely is exactly the content type that would eventually hit that ceiling, so past events are archived out on a schedule rather than kept forever. Build the archiving job at the same time as the calendar, not after it becomes a problem.

Service notes that affect the build

Follow Up Boss

RegistrationFollow Up Boss asks that anyone using the API register their system, and requires it when the API is used to provide services to one of their customers, which is our situation. Registration returns headers sent on every request. It also raises the request allowance considerably. Apply early.
Sending leadsLeads go to the events endpoint, not the people endpoint. The documentation is explicit that creating people directly skips automations and causes duplicates. The events endpoint deduplicates on phone and email and triggers the team's lead routing.
The silent failureA 204 response means the lead flow for that source is archived and the lead was accepted but ignored. It is not an error, and it will look like success unless handled. Log and alert on it.
Event types matterOnly certain event types trigger action plans and automations. Choosing the right type is the difference between a lead entering a nurture sequence and sitting untouched.
Rate limitsSending leads is effectively unlimited. The limits apply to reading data back, which this site barely does. A 429 can arrive even when the remaining count suggests capacity, so honour the retry header rather than our own arithmetic.
PixelInstall for activity tracking, but turn form capture off, since our forms post server-side. Running both creates duplicate leads. The Pixel also cannot read forms inside embedded frames, which is one reason the later MLS phase is planned as native pages.

Sanity

Read configurationPublic page reads must go through the cached CDN endpoint. Direct API reads cost roughly ten times more per request, and a misconfigured client is the standard way to turn a free project into an expensive one.
TierThe free tier covers this site comfortably. Live preview and visual editing are included on it, which is unusual and worth using from day one. The upgrade triggers are private datasets, scheduled publishing, and the wider role model, none of which are needed at launch.
PublishingA webhook fires on publish and refreshes only the affected pages. Content appears in seconds without a deployment.
SchedulingScheduled publishing is a paid feature, and the calendar does not need it. Events carry dates and the site filters out past ones.
AI actionsAvailable and useful, but flagged experimental and requiring a specific API version. Fine for optional tooling, not for anything on the launch path.

Glossary

Headless CMS
A content system with no website of its own. It stores the content and hands it to whatever site asks for it, which is why the site can be redesigned without touching the content.
Schema
The definition of what fields a listing or an event has. It lives in the code, so it is versioned and reviewable.
Draft
A saved but unpublished version. Nothing a visitor can see.
Pixel
A small script that reports visitor activity to the CRM so a lead's browsing history is attached to their record.
Event (CRM sense)
A recorded action by a lead, such as a form submission or a property view. Different from a calendar event, which is a cultural happening in the area. The two share a word and nothing else.
Webhook
An automatic message from one service to another when something happens.
Revalidation
Refreshing only the pages that actually changed, instead of rebuilding the whole site.
10DLC
The carrier registration required to send business text messages reliably. It checks the business details and the website's consent language.
All-party consent
The rule in Florida that everyone on a call must agree before it is recorded, unlike states where one party's consent is enough.
IDX / MLS
The licensed feed of listings from the local board, carrying rules about refresh frequency and attribution.
