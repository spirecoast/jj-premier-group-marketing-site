The templates, in fourteen families

Built the way the Virtus set was built: fourteen families, each with one job from the social plan, ten templates in each. The registers are round three's (Gallery, Golden Hour, Big Type, Harbor Night, Poster, Cut Paper, Elevation, Cinema, Chart Room, Quiet, Coldwell Banker Blue, Edition), carried into formats round three didn't have: checklists, dates, corrections, this-or-that, text threads, carousels and stories.

The writing is the point of this round. Every line talks to one person, plainly. Are you thinking about moving? If you think someone would find this useful, please share it. No exclamation marks, no superlatives, nothing described as stunning or a must-see. Every number carries its source and pull date. Every house is described, never the people who might live in it. Venues are named properly and sell nothing.

Under each image is the line on the image, a suggested caption written in the same voice, and what to swap or check before it posts.

Seven things to confirm

  1. Listings are samples. Every listing, open house, sold and showing piece uses an invented house and price on a real neighborhood name, and says “Sample listing · for review” on the piece. Swap in real listings and their own photos, then remove that line.
  2. Market figures. Medians, counts and shares come from the county appraiser files in the repo (data/sales): qualified home sales, October 2025 to September 2026, pulled October 2026. The month in progress is never complete. Re-run the numbers the week a figure posts.
  3. Encore. Every show, venue and date was checked against the Encore calendar in the repo on October 24, 2026. Check each one again at the venue's own site the week it posts, and never imply a sponsorship.
  4. Brokerage sign-off. “Coldwell Banker Realty” appears on all 140 pieces. Listing pieces carry the Equal Housing mark. The franchise logo appears only in the Coldwell Banker Blue register. Nothing ships without the brokerage contact's review.

  6. Stickers. Poll, question, link and countdown stickers on the stories are drawn placeholders. Add the real Instagram stickers when posting.

Before You

10 templates

Three or four things to do before you list, offer, close, or buy behind a gate. The lists are short on purpose; the save is the point, and the caption asks people to share it if it would help someone.

04-01Before you list

Three things to do about eight weeks before you list. (1) Walk the house with us and write down what a buyer will notice first. (2) Book the photographer. The good ones fill up six weeks ahead. (3) Find your insurance, flood and permit paperwork now, not the week of closing.

Caption Eight weeks is about right. It's enough time to fix the things that would cost you at inspection and to get the photographer you actually want. If you think someone would find this useful, please share it.

04-02Before you make an offer

Four things to ask before you make an offer on this coast. (1) The flood zone, and whether there's an elevation certificate. (2) The year the roof was replaced. Your insurer will ask. (3) What the association, and any district, actually charges. (4) What the last three houses on the street sold for.

Caption These four answers change what a house costs to own here, not just to buy. We'll get them for you before you write anything. Save this for when you need it.

04-03Before hurricane season

Before hurricane season, do these three things. (1) Photograph every room and the outside, for insurance. Phone photos are fine. (2) Look up your evacuation zone. It is not the same as your flood zone. (3) Find the water shutoff and know where the shutters are, before you need them.

Caption Three things to do before June 1 that take an afternoon. Your evacuation zone and your flood zone are different maps; look up both. If you think someone would find this useful, please share it.

04-04Before you buy a condo

Three documents to read before you buy a condo here. (1) The milestone inspection report, if the building is three stories or more. (2) The reserve study, and whether the reserves are actually funded. (3) The last two years of board minutes. That's where the real story is.

Caption Since 2022, most condo buildings here have to have a milestone inspection and a reserve study. Read both, then read the board minutes. We'll help you get them. Save this.

04-05Before closing week

Three things to do the week you close. (1) Confirm wire instructions by phone, calling a number you already had. Never from a new email. (2) Do the walk-through the day before, and run every tap. (3) Have the utilities put in your name for the day of closing.

Caption Wire fraud is real and it targets closings. Call the title company on a number you already had, not one from an email that just arrived. We'll remind you again that week.

04-06Before a gated community

Four things to ask before you buy in a gated community. (1) Who runs the gate: the association, or a district you also pay. (2) What the fees cover, and what they don't. Roofs and roads are the big ones. (3) The rules on renting, fences, boats and trucks in the driveway. (4) Whether there's a special assessment coming. Ask it exactly that way.

Caption Gates come with rules and bills. None of them are deal-breakers if you know them going in. We wrote a longer guide to this on our site; this is the short version.

04-07Before you move from out of state

Three things to do before you move here from somewhere else. (1) Get an insurance quote on a house before you fall for it. Here, that's a real number. (2) Do the homestead math. Filing by March 1 can change your tax bill for years. (3) Write down a hurricane plan now, while it's calm and nobody's worried.

Caption If you're moving here from another state, these three things will save you more than any negotiation. If you think someone would find this useful, please share it.

04-08Before an open house

Three things to do before your open house. (1) Lights on, blinds up, every door open. Buyers won't open a closed door. (2) Put the dog somewhere kind. A nervous dog makes nervous buyers. (3) Leave for the whole window. People say what they really think when the owner isn't there.

Caption Sellers always ask what to do before an open house. These three, and then go get lunch. We'll call you the minute it ends.

04-09Before you sell from away

Three things to sort out before you sell a house you don't live in. (1) A key with a neighbor or with us, and a list of who's allowed in. (2) The water off at the main, and the air conditioning left on. This is Florida. (3) Tell your insurer the house is vacant. Many policies change after thirty or sixty days empty.

Caption Selling a house from another state is normal here; about a third of our sellers do it. These three things prevent most of the problems. Save it for when you need it.

04-10Before a showing

Three things to do at a showing that most people skip. (1) Drive the street at the hour you'd actually be coming home. (2) Open the electrical panel and look at the water heater's date. (3) Stand in the backyard for a full minute and just listen.

Caption A showing lasts twenty minutes. These three take five of them and tell you more than the kitchen does. If you think someone would find this useful, please share it.

How it was built

Each template is HTML and CSS rendered by Chromium at 1080 px wide (4:5, square, or 9:16). The deck of copy, photos and registers is design/social-templates/deck-a.mjs and deck-b.mjs; render.mjs draws them; this page is built from the same deck.

Copy rules kept

Team voice (“we”), never one agent's first person. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no superlatives, no “reach out”. Fair housing: the property, never the people. The tagline is not rewritten and never used as a headline.

Tide figures were recomputed from data/sales for this round (single-family medians: Lakewood Ranch $730,000, Sarasota $619,700, Bradenton $435,000). Atlas maps plot the 2,038 neighborhoods with coordinates in the Atlas dataset. Encore picks come from the site's calendar file.
