# Illustration prompts · JJ Premier Group

Written for Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image). Every prompt is a self-contained scene in the New Yorker cover tradition: one witty idea, hand-drawn line, flat gouache and ink, a limited palette, and room to breathe. Paste the house style block first, then the prompt. Sixty prompts across five sets: Encore key art, Atlas places, Tide covers, the site and brand, and a social series built to travel.

## 0 · How to prompt this model

What Google's own guide asks for, applied here.

- **Describe the scene, don't list keywords.** Every prompt below is a paragraph with a subject, an action, a setting, light and mood. Add to it in sentences.
- **Be hyper-specific and say what the image is for.** The purpose is stated in each prompt ("a magazine cover for…", "key art for a calendar tile…"). Keep it; the model composes differently for a cover than for a sticker.
- **Semantic negatives.** Never write "no people". Write what you want instead: "an empty promenade at dawn", "the only figures are birds". The prompts already do this.
- **Control the camera.** Use composition language: "eye level", "bird's-eye", "a single subject in the lower third", "generous negative space at the top for a masthead".
- **Text.** When a prompt asks for lettering, the exact words are in quotes and the type is named ("hand-lettered serif in the manner of a magazine masthead"). Everywhere else the prompt asks for a scene with nothing to read, because stray letters are the most common flaw.
- **Set ratio and size as parameters, not prose.** Supported aspect ratios: 1:1, 3:2, 2:3, 3:4, 4:3, 4:5, 5:4, 9:16, 16:9, 21:9. Sizes: 1K, 2K, 4K (uppercase K). Web slots take 2K; print and the hero take 4K. Each prompt lists its ratio.
- **References.** Pro accepts up to 14 reference images: up to 6 objects, 5 characters and 3 style references. Generate the style plate (prompt 1) and the cast sheet (prompt 2) first, then attach them as style and character references to every later prompt. That is how sixty images become one collection.
- **Iterate in the conversation.** "Keep everything, make the water a shade lighter" works. Regenerating from scratch loses the composition you liked.
- **Thinking.** Leave thinking at its default (high) for the multi-element covers; drop to minimal for tiles with one subject if you are batching.

## 1 · The house style block

Paste this first, every time. It is the only place the palette is written.

> Editorial cover illustration in the tradition of The New Yorker magazine covers: one witty visual idea told without words, confident hand-drawn ink line with variable weight, flat gouache color laid in loosely with visible brush texture and the occasional gap where the paper shows, no gradients, no outlines around every shape, no drop shadows. Limited palette drawn from the brand: navy #2e4a5c and deep harbor #1e3442 for ink and the darkest shapes; linen #f3eee6 and parchment #f1e9dc for paper and light; amber #96702a as the single warm accent, used once or twice; teal #35899c and mist #c7e7ef for water and sky, never more than a sliver; sand #d2c6b4 for beaches and roofs; graphite #53565a for line work. Warm paper grain over the whole image. Generous negative space; one calm area left open. Deadpan, affectionate humor; nothing cartoonish, nothing cute for its own sake. Straight horizons. The only living figures are the birds and animals of the Florida Gulf Coast, drawn with character; any people are small, seen from behind or at a distance, varied, and never the subject. Nothing in the image is readable as text unless this prompt says otherwise.

## 2 · The cast

The coast's animals carry the wit, so no image ever says who lives here. Generate this sheet once and attach it as a character reference.

**Prompt 2 · The cast sheet** (4:3, 2K)
> A character model sheet for an illustrated series, six animals of Florida's Gulf Coast drawn in the house style on a single parchment page, each shown twice (standing and in motion), with plenty of white space between them and nothing written. PELL, a brown pelican with a heavy bill, a slightly weary patrician expression and a narrow navy bow tie, the arts patron of the group. AUGUSTA, a great blue heron, tall, precise, standing on one leg, a surveyor's air, carrying a rolled map under one wing. MO, a manatee, round, unhurried, permanently content, floating with a folded newspaper on her belly. THE CRANES, a pair of sandhill cranes with red caps who walk everywhere side by side like an old married couple and stop traffic doing it. ROSIE, a roseate spoonbill, pink, theatrical, always mid-gesture. HENRY, a gopher tortoise, slow, thorough, a small reading lamp on his shell. Ink line, flat gouache, brand palette, paper grain.

## 3 · Encore key art (the arts calendar)

One image per category and the subcategories that occur most. Each is event-agnostic so it can stand behind any listing. Ratio 16:9 for the calendar tile; regenerate at 4:5 for the phone card and 1:1 for social. 2K.

3. **Music · concert** — A red-and-gold opera house seen from the stage, house lights half up, the rows of red velvet seats empty except for PELL the pelican alone in the front row of the balcony, program open on his lap, waiting with enormous patience. One amber spotlight catches the dust in the air. The stage floor in the foreground is bare boards, left empty for type. No readable text anywhere in the hall.
4. **Music · orchestra** — Bird's-eye view straight down onto an orchestra's empty chairs and music stands arranged in their arcs on a pale stage, the conductor's podium at the center, one cello case open, and AUGUSTA the heron standing at the podium with a baton, one leg raised, ready. The arcs of chairs are the pattern; the rest of the frame is stage floor.
5. **Music · jazz** — A small dark club at night, one amber lamp on a round table, a double bass leaning against a chair, cigarette-free air, and through the window the Sarasota bayfront in navy blue hour. On the bandstand a trio of cranes and a spoonbill mid-number, silhouetted against the one lit wall. The floor and ceiling are deep navy; the lamp is the only warmth.
6. **Music · choral** — A church nave with tall clear windows and afternoon light in long bands across the pews, a choir loft at the back, and on the loft rail a row of twelve identical pelicans in navy robes, beaks open in unison, one of them a half beat late. The pews are empty and the light does the work.
7. **Theater · play** — The proscenium of a Mediterranean-revival theater, red curtain caught in mid-rise by two sandhill cranes pulling the rope from the wings, one small set piece on stage: a kitchen table and one chair under a single work light. The audience is dark and empty. Amber on the curtain fringe only.
8. **Theater · musical** — The same proscenium, curtain fully up, a staircase set in navy and cream, and ROSIE the spoonbill at the top of the stairs in a spotlight with one wing flung wide, feathers as a fan. Confetti drifts in the air as tiny pale flecks. Marquee bulbs frame the stage, unlit.
9. **Theater · comedy and improv** — A black-box theater, four folding chairs on a bare stage, a single microphone stand, and HENRY the tortoise at the mic, deadpan, while the front row (three pelicans) is doubled over. Work lights, no color except one amber gel.
10. **Theater · dance and ballet** — A rehearsal studio with a long mirror and a barre, morning light from tall windows, and the pair of sandhill cranes at the barre in fifth position, reflected perfectly, a rosin box on the floor. Pale floor, navy mirror frame, the cranes' red caps the only warm note besides the sun.
11. **Theater · circus** — The Sarasota circus tradition: a striped tent interior in navy and cream, a single trapeze swinging in an empty ring, sawdust, and PELL on the trapeze in his bow tie, entirely unbothered, mid-swing. The tent's peak is dark; the ring is lit.
12. **Galleries · exhibition** — A white-walled gallery on a quiet weekday, polished concrete floor, one large abstract canvas of the Gulf in teal and sand, and AUGUSTA the heron standing before it for a very long time, head tilted exactly as the painting's heron is tilted. The bench is empty. North light.
13. **Galleries · opening reception** — The same gallery at night from the street, the front window glowing warm, a crowd inside seen only as backs and raised glasses, small and varied, and outside on the sidewalk under a palm the two cranes reading the poster in the window, which is a blank amber rectangle. Wet pavement reflects the window.
14. **Talks · lecture** — A lecture hall's raked seats from the back, a lectern with a small reading lamp, a blank projection screen, and HENRY the tortoise at the lectern with a stack of notes taller than he is. Every seat holds a small attentive pelican. The screen's rectangle is left empty for type.
15. **Talks · author and book** — A bookshop's back room with mismatched chairs, shelves to the ceiling, a small table with a water glass and a stack of one navy book with a blank cover, and MO the manatee somehow in the reading chair, spectacles on, holding the room. Warm lamplight, evening.
16. **Film · screening** — A single-screen art-house cinema seen from the projection booth, the beam of light cutting through the dark, the screen a bright empty rectangle, and the audience a scatter of silhouettes among which one pelican head rises above the rest. Dust in the beam; amber exit light.
17. **Film · outdoor** — Movies on the lawn at dusk: a screen on a frame in a park by the bay, blankets in navy and sand on the grass with small figures seen from behind, string lights, and the cranes standing at the back because they never sit. The screen is a blank pale rectangle; the sky is blue hour.
18. **Festivals · street festival** — A downtown street closed to cars, bunting in navy, sand and amber crossing overhead between live oaks, white tents down both sides, and at the center a single pelican processing down the middle of the street as if leading a parade no one told him about. Bird's-eye from a second-floor balcony. The street surface is left open.
19. **Festivals · art walk** — Painted 1920s cottages of an arts village, hand-painted trim in the brand palette, easels along the sidewalk with blank canvases, jasmine on a fence, and ROSIE the spoonbill posing beside an easel as if she were the work. Late afternoon, long shadows.
20. **Family · program** — A library's children's room, low round tables, a rug in navy and sand stripes, picture books face-out with blank covers, and a storytime circle of sandhill crane chicks around HENRY the tortoise with his reading lamp. Morning light through a window with a palm outside.
21. **Family · outdoors** — A nature-center boardwalk over a mangrove tunnel, dappled light, a small group seen from behind at the rail, small and varied, looking down at MO the manatee looking up. The boardwalk's planks run to the vanishing point.
22. **Markets · farmers market** — A Saturday farmers market under white tents on a brick street at eight in the morning, crates of mangos, tomatoes and bread, cut flowers in buckets, and the cranes walking the aisle inspecting produce with the seriousness of judges. Awnings scalloped in navy and cream. The foreground brick is empty.
23. **Markets · art and antiques market** — Folding tables of blue glass, brass lamps and framed prints under live oaks in a park, and AUGUSTA the heron examining a very small painting through a very large magnifying glass. Dappled light, no readable labels.

## 4 · Atlas (places, never people)

Places drawn as places. Nothing in these implies who lives anywhere; the houses are empty and the streets belong to the birds. Ratio 16:9 for market and area heroes, 3:2 for cards. 2K, 4K for the three market heroes.

24. **Atlas · the map itself** — A bird's-eye view of a hand-drawn map of the coast between the Manatee River and Sarasota Bay, lakes and canals in teal, villages as clusters of tiny tile roofs in sand, live-oak canopies as soft navy blobs, the barrier islands as thin pale lines, and AUGUSTA the heron standing on the map itself with one foot on a village, consulting her rolled chart. The map's paper is parchment; there are no place names, only shapes.
25. **Lakewood Ranch · hero** — A planned community from a low bird's-eye at four in the afternoon: a chain of lakes in teal, tile roofs in sand around them, preserve in deep navy-green, a roundabout with a fountain at the center, and on the roundabout's lawn the pair of sandhill cranes holding up a line of very patient cars with nowhere to be. Long shadows east. The lake in the lower third is calm and open for type.
26. **Lakewood Ranch · Main Street at dawn** — A Mediterranean-revival main street under live oaks, brick pavers, cafe chairs still stacked, string lights just going off, the storefronts' signs blank pale rectangles, and PELL walking the empty center line like he owns it. Low warm light from the east, cool shade under the oaks.
27. **Lakewood Ranch · Waterside on a Sunday** — A lakefront promenade at dusk, string lights on, a farmers market packing up, paddleboards on a calm lake, small figures from behind, and MO the manatee surfacing beside the dock to see what the fuss is. Sky mist to navy.
28. **Lakewood Ranch · the golf village** — A fairway seen from a screened lanai in the foreground (the screen drawn as a fine grid), a bunker like a white comma, a lake, a distant tile roof, and a gopher tortoise crossing the green at his own pace while a foursome of pelicans waits politely. Morning.
29. **Sarasota · hero** — The bayfront at blue hour from the water: a purple seashell-shaped concert hall small but unmistakable on the shore, moored sailboats with bare masts, downtown windows lit softly, glass-calm water mirroring everything, and one pelican asleep on a piling in the foreground. Navy sky, mist horizon, amber windows.
30. **Sarasota · the museum courtyard** — A long formal courtyard of an Italianate museum, columns and loggias in cream, a bronze figure at the far end seen as a silhouette, banyan roots at the edges, and ROSIE the spoonbill on the lawn striking the same pose as the statue. Midday shade, bright courtyard.
31. **Sarasota · the circle on the key** — A round shopping circle with a park at its center, palms, a boutique's striped awning in navy and cream, a vintage convertible parked with no one in it, and the two cranes crossing at the crosswalk, traffic stopped. Bright afternoon, long palm shadows.
32. **Siesta · the quartz sand** — A beach of white powder sand so bright it reads as paper, a teal Gulf, a lifeguard stand painted navy, and a line of beach umbrellas in the palette receding to the point; in the foreground a pelican lies on his back in the sand as if it were a spa. Noon, minimal shadow.
33. **Sarasota · the banyan garden** — A bayfront botanical garden: an enormous banyan with a hundred hanging roots, orchids in its crook, a shell path, the bay bright beyond, and HENRY the tortoise deep in the roots reading under his lamp. Green kept to navy and teal, never bright green.
34. **Bradenton · hero** — The Riverwalk along the Manatee River at golden hour, the curve of the walk, a pier in silhouette, a skate park's bowl empty, the river glass, and three manatees surfacing in a row like commas in the water. Warm horizon, navy sky above. The river in the lower third is open for type.
35. **Bradenton · the arts village** — Brightly painted 1920s cottages converted to studios, each in one brand color, picket fences, a mural of a spoonbill on one wall (painted flat, no words), jasmine, and the real ROSIE standing beside her portrait for comparison. Late afternoon.
36. **Bradenton · the canal streets** — A mid-century Florida ranch on a canal, terrazzo glimpsed through open sliders, a small boat at the dock, a screened pool cage, and MO drifting past the dock at the pace of the evening. Twilight, interior lamps warm, sky deep navy.
37. **Bradenton · the fishing village** — A working fishing village at dawn: shrimp boats with nets up, stacked crab traps in sand and navy, a fish house on stilts, pelicans lined on every piling waiting for the boats, one of them PELL in his bow tie among the commoners. Mist on the water.
38. **Anna Maria · the island** — A low island street with pastel cottages in parchment, sand and mist, a free trolley the color of navy with no wording, bicycles with baskets leaned on a fence, sea oats, and the Gulf at the end of the street. The cranes ride the trolley. Height limit kept: nothing taller than a palm.
39. **The bridge** — The Sunshine Skyway from the water at sunrise, its cables a fan of fine lines in amber light, the bay flat mist, and one pelican gliding under the span at exactly the right moment. Ninety percent sky and water.
40. **The 3 p.m. rain** — A residential street of tile roofs under a wall of navy summer rain arriving from the east while the west half of the street is still in sun, a rainbow beginning, sprinklers still on out of habit, and MO in a canal enjoying the one thing she is built for. Split light, half warm half cool.

## 5 · Tide (the newsletter)

Monthly covers with room for a masthead. Ratio 4:5 for email and print, 16:9 for the web band. 2K for email, 4K for print. Each leaves the top fifth open. Lettering is specified where it belongs; otherwise none.

41. **Tide · the masthead plate** — A calm bay at dusk seen straight on, water in mist and teal with soft ripples, a thin line of horizon, sky from linen to navy, one pelican on one piling at the far right, and across the top fifth, hand-lettered in a high-contrast serif in the manner of a magazine masthead, the single word "Tide" in navy. Nothing else written. (4:5)
42. **October · the season opens** — PELL at his dressing-room mirror lit by round bulbs, straightening his bow tie with one wing, a program on the table, the opera house's red curtain visible through the open door. Amber bulbs on navy. The top fifth is the ceiling, open.
43. **November · the snowbirds land** — A V of white birds descending over the bay toward a row of condo balconies, each bird carrying a tiny suitcase in its beak; on the ground the cranes hold a handwritten sign that is a blank sand rectangle. Cool sky, warm balconies.
44. **December · the boat parade** — A canal at night lined with docks, boats strung with lights in amber and mist, reflections doubling everything, families seen from behind on the docks, small and varied, and MO wearing one string of lights, floating at the end of the parade at her own pace. Navy water, no other color but the lights.
45. **January · high season** — A restaurant patio on a circle at eight in the evening, every table full of backs and shoulders, waiters carrying trays, string lights, and at the host stand a pelican holding a clipboard with a very long blank list. The sky is navy; the patio glows.
46. **February · spring training** — A small ballpark at golden hour, the diamond's chalk bright, palms beyond the outfield wall, a scoreboard that is a blank navy rectangle, and the sandhill cranes standing at second base refusing to move for the groundskeeper. Bleachers full of small varied figures from behind.
47. **March · the tourist season peaks** — A beach access path between sea oats at noon, a line of umbrellas beyond, a parking sign that is a blank panel, and AUGUSTA the heron directing traffic with the authority of someone who was here first. Bright, minimal shadow.
48. **April · film festival** — A red carpet rolled down the sidewalk of a downtown theater at dusk, marquee bulbs lit around a blank marquee, velvet rope, and ROSIE the spoonbill on the carpet giving the photographers (three pelicans with cameras) everything they came for. Wet pavement reflections.
49. **May · mango season** — A backyard mango tree heavy with fruit in amber and sand over a screened lanai, a ladder, a basket, and MO in the pool below waiting for one to fall, HENRY the tortoise already eating one. Bright morning; the fruit is the only amber.
50. **June · the storms** — Late afternoon, a towering navy cumulus over the Gulf, the beach emptying, umbrellas folding, and one pelican standing his ground on a piling, entirely unimpressed. The cloud takes the upper two-thirds; the masthead sits in it.
51. **July · the quiet months** — A main street at noon in July, heat shimmer drawn as wavering lines, cafe chairs empty, a "closed for August" sign that is a blank card in a window, and the cranes in the shade of a live oak with a cooler, having the town to themselves.
52. **August · turtle nesting** — A beach at night under a navy sky full of stars, a roped-off nest with a small sand sign, tiny turtles heading for a moonlit Gulf, and PELL holding a shielded amber lantern low, escorting them. The only light is the moon and the lantern.
53. **September · the coast holds its breath** — A harbor with every boat tied double, shutters closed on a pastel cottage, a sky of long quiet clouds, and MO in the still water with the newspaper, unbothered, as she always is. Muted palette, sand and mist, the calmest cover of the year.

## 6 · The site and the brand

Hero, page and card slots that the photography does not cover, drawn instead. Ratios as noted. 4K for the hero and the print pieces.

60. **404 · wrong turn** (1:1) — A roundabout in the middle of a preserve with four exits and no signs (blank posts), and PELL standing at the center on the fountain's rim, wings out, looking in every direction at once. Bright and empty.
61. **Share card plate** (16:9) — A calm bay at blue hour with a single piling and a single pelican at the far right, ninety percent sky and water in mist to navy, the left two-thirds completely open, made to carry a title and the wordmark. 4K.
62. **Holiday card** (5:4) — The cast on a dock at dusk in December, each holding one warm lantern, the water doubling the lights, a small boat behind them strung in amber, and the cranes, of course, standing. Navy and amber only.

## 8 · Production notes

- Generate 1 and 2 first, at 2K. Keep the best style plate and cast sheet as references for everything after; attach the plate as a style reference and the sheet as a character reference on every call.
- Batch by set. Within a set, run with the same seed of description and change only the scene, so the ink weight and paper grain stay constant.
- Where the calendar needs a ground for type, the prompts already leave one (a bare stage floor, a calm lower third, an empty street). Do not add gradients in post; extend the paper instead.
- Landmarks are described by form, never named in the image, and no image carries a logo. Nothing here depicts a specific private home.
- Fair housing: the only recurring figures are animals. Where people appear they are small, varied, and seen from behind. Nothing in this collection should be edited to show who lives in a place.
- File the keepers under `public/images/illustration/` at 2560px on the long side as JPEG, register their dimensions in `lib/content/image-dims.ts`, and the site's image component delivers them as AVIF.
