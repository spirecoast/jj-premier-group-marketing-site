Page 4 · What things cost
The money you need that is not the deposit
Closing costs on the Suncoast run roughly two to five percent of the purchase price, and they are separate from your down payment. On a $450,000 house that is $9,000 to $22,500.
The big items are title insurance, the state documentary stamp, prepaid property tax, prepaid insurance, and lender fees. Your inspection and appraisal are paid before closing, out of pocket.

Page 5 · One word explained
Escrow
A neutral third party holds your deposit and the paperwork until both sides have done what they promised. It is not the seller's account and it is not ours. If the deal falls apart for a reason your contract allows, the money comes back to you from escrow.
You will hear it used three ways: the account, the period between contract and closing, and the company handling it. All three are normal.

Page 6 · What returns the money
Paint, light and the front door
Interior paint, updated light fixtures and a repainted front door are the three cheapest changes that move a price on this coast. A new kitchen almost never returns its cost in under two years, and we will tell you so before you spend it.
Page 9 · What we do not do
We will not overprice it to win the listing
Some agents will quote you a number they know is too high to get the signature, then spend six weeks talking you down. If our number is lower than someone else's, ask them for the four comparable sales that support theirs.
Page 11 · Storm season
What a named storm does to a closing
Insurance binding stops when a storm is named, which can pause a closing until the system clears. Nobody enjoys this conversation in July, which is exactly why it has a page.
Page 14 · The offer table
The highest offer is not always the best one
Financing type, inspection period, and whether the buyer has a house to sell all matter more than the last ten thousand dollars. We will show you every offer side by side in one table.
03 · How the two differ
Linen explains, Harbor assumes
The buyer guide defines every term and runs two to four sentences a page. The seller guide runs one to two and skips the tour of the obvious.
No prices on the cover
Guides outlive their figures. Every number lives in the body with a dated footnote, so a reprint is a text change and not a redesign.
Say the unhelpful thing
The kitchen page and the overpricing page are the two spreads clients remember, because no competitor's brochure contains them.
One photograph per guide
The duo shot on the inside back cover, and nothing else. These are documents, not brochures, and the restraint is what makes them read as useful.
Before printing
Every figure in these specimens is illustrative. Closing costs, insurance and documentary stamp rates change, so each guide needs a dated footer and a review each January. Have a Florida real estate attorney read the cost and contract pages before the first print run, and keep the guides free of anything that could read as legal, tax or insurance advice.
