Brand voice How we sound in words
Brand voice
Anyone can copy the palette. Nobody can copy the way Joelyn and Jessica talk about a house. This page exists so the writing sounds like the two of them at a kitchen island, not like a listing portal.
01
The voice
We sound like the friend who happens to know the neighborhood: specific, a little opinionated, and genuinely glad you asked.
Warm
We like these houses and it shows. Enthusiasm lives in the detail we choose, so the sentence about the kitchen window at 4pm does more work than any adjective could.
Specific
A number, a street, or a date in almost every paragraph. Details are the fun part and the proof at the same time: rebuilt in 2019, nine days on market, sidewalks on both sides.
Local
We know the water table, the flood zone, the season, and where to eat after a showing. Nineteen years on the Suncoast buys opinions worth having.
02
Words we like
Concrete nouns, real figures, and the occasional strong opinion.
the good end of the street

the sold price, not the list

Notice what these have in common: every one of them could only be said by somebody who has actually stood in the house. That is the whole trick. Specificity reads as warmth, because it proves we paid attention.
Skip these
stunning
must see
won't last
dream home
luxury lifestyle
hidden gem
priced to sell
reach out
let's chat
exclusive opportunity
Not because they are forbidden, because they are empty. Every one describes a feeling the reader has not had yet. Say the thing that caused the feeling instead. Two house rules while we are here: no exclamation marks, no emoji.
03
Sharper
Same facts, better sentence. The right column is the one to copy.
Flat
Stunning waterfront estate offering the ultimate Florida lifestyle. This must-see dream home won't last long.
Ours
Water views from three levels, a kitchen rebuilt in 2019, and the good end of the street. Four similar homes sold here last year, all inside nine days.
Flat
Thinking of selling? Let's chat about how I can help you maximize your home's value in today's hot market.
Ours
Thinking about spring? Start in October. Prep takes eight weeks and the good photographers book six ahead.
Flat
Price improvement. Seller is motivated and this exclusive opportunity is priced to sell fast.
Ours
Down to $1,150,000 from $1,295,000. Thirty-one days told us the first number was wrong, so we moved it.
Flat
Perfect starter home for a young family in a friendly, safe neighborhood.
Ours
Three bedrooms, a fenced yard, and a nine-minute walk to the elementary school. Sidewalks on both sides.
FAIR HOUSING: DESCRIBE THE PROPERTY, NOT THE PEOPLE
Flat
Hi. Thanks so much for reaching out. I would love to help you find your dream home. When can we hop on a quick call?
Ours
Good to hear from you. Two questions before we look at anything: when do you need to be in, and is there a house to sell first? Those two answers change everything else.
04
Two registers
Same voice, different amount of explaining.
Linen-led
First-time buyers
Two to four sentences, and generous with them. Define every term the first time it appears: escrow, contingency, closing costs, homestead. Say what happens next and on which day. Nobody is born knowing this and pretending otherwise is the fastest way to lose a first-timer.
EXAMPLE
"Your first one is the one we explain twice."
Harbor-led
Luxury
One or two sentences. Assume fluency, skip the tour of the obvious, and land on the single number that matters. Discretion is the product at this level, so the writing holds back more than it hands over.
EXAMPLE
"Discretion, and the right eleven buyers."
05
By channel
How many words each surface gets, and what it may ask for.
Yard sign
Two words and a phone number. The panel says who, a rider says what changed. Never a sentence.
2 TO 4 WORDS · NO CTA
Business card
Name, role, one number, one email, license. The tagline is the only copy, and no claim would fit anyway.
TAGLINE ONLY
Listing copy
Three sentences: what is unusual, what was rebuilt and when, what the street is like. Then the numbers in mono. One adjective per sentence, maximum.
45 TO 60 WORDS
Social · quote
One idea, no ask. These are the posts that earn the right to post the promotions.
UNDER 20 WORDS
Social · culture
About the venue, never the listing. No price, no address, no call to action, no Coldwell Banker mark. Name the institution properly and say why you like it.
SELLS NOTHING
Market letter
One page a quarter, no pitch. Lead with the number that surprised us, name the streets, and own whatever we got wrong last time.
400 WORDS MAX
First reply
Two questions back rather than a pitch: when do you need to be in, and is there a house to sell first. Everything else follows from those.
ASK BEFORE YOU OFFER
06
The tagline
The one line nobody rewrites.
Tagline
Every move, expertly guided.
SET IN  IvyPresto Headline Thin Italic 800
ONE LINE  in lockups, plates, footers
SQUARE CROPS  may break once, after "move,"
NEVER  all caps, mid-clause breaks, or Sky 300
Sentence case, keep the period
The full stop is doing real work. It makes the line sound settled instead of shouted, and dropping it turns a promise into a slogan.
Never a headline
It closes a surface rather than opening one. Where there is a real headline, the tagline sits at the foot, small.
Never adapted
No seasonal variants, no "expertly guided since 2019." The line stays fixed and the copy around it does the flexing.
07
Four hard lines
Not style preferences. These ones carry real exposure.
Fair housing
Describe the property, not the people. Nothing that references race, religion, national origin, sex, familial status, or disability, and nothing implying who a neighborhood would suit. "Perfect for a young family" and "safe area" both cross the line. Say the fenced yard and the sidewalk instead.
Claims and figures
Every number gets a source and a date or it does not run. No "#1" or "top producing" without naming the ranking body and the year. Never predict a future price. Square footage and lot size come from public record, not from the seller.
Franchise and license
The Coldwell Banker mark runs per franchise rules with no accompanying wording. Florida license numbers (DBPR, SL…) appear on cards, signage, and email signatures. Brokerage identification rules vary by state, so confirm before publishing anywhere new.
Venues and partners
Culture posts name real institutions, so get them right: correct name, correct venue, current season. Never imply a sponsorship or affiliation the team does not have. Check show titles and dates against the venue's own calendar before scheduling.
