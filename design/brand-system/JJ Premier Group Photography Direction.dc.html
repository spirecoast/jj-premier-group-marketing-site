Photography direction
Photography decides whether this brand reads as considered or as a template. Every layout in the system leaves a generous image slot, and a single over-processed HDR frame undoes all of it. This is what to brief a photographer.
NATURAL LIGHT ONLY
NO FLASH, NO HDR, NO SKY REPLACEMENT
LENS  24MM AT WIDEST, 35MM PREFERRED
VERTICALS STRAIGHT, ALWAYS
DELIVERY  3:2 · 4:5 · 16:9 · 1:1
SHOT TO CROP, NOT CROPPED AFTER
01
The look
Warm daylight, straight verticals, rooms that look lived in. If a frame could pass for a hotel brochure, it is wrong for this brand.
Golden hour, not midday
Exteriors an hour before sunset, interiors mid-morning. Florida midday sun blows out a white wall and turns a pool into a mirror.
Lived in, not staged empty
A book on the counter, a chair pulled out. Empty rooms photograph as square footage; occupied rooms photograph as a house.
Cool shadow, warm light
The palette lives in the images: Harbor blues in the shade, Linen warmth in the sun. Grade toward that, never toward teal and orange.
02 · The three subjects
Property · 3:2 and 4:5
Property
The hero exterior at golden hour, then the kitchen, then whatever is actually unusual about the house. Twelve frames beat forty.
SHOT LIST  EXTERIOR HERO · TWILIGHT
KITCHEN · PRIMARY BATH · THE VIEW
ONE DETAIL THAT PROVES QUALITY
DRONE ONLY IF THE LOT EXPLAINS THE PRICE
People
Eye level, mid-conversation, hands doing something. Both agents on a real property, never in a studio and never in front of a car.
EACH AGENT  VERTICAL · HALF-BODY · SEATED
DUO  SQUARE AND ULTRAWIDE
LIGHT AND DARK GROUND OF EACH
NO CROSSED ARMS, NO BLAZER OVER SHOULDER
Place · 16:9 and 1:1
Place
The library the culture posts need. Waterside on a Sunday, the Manatee river, the oaks on Main Street. Shot on the team's own phone is fine here, and better than stock.
BUILD 40 FRAMES ACROSS FOUR MARKETS
NO IDENTIFIABLE FACES WITHOUT A RELEASE
NO STOCK IMAGERY, EVER
03 · Brief for this
✓A kitchen at 4pm with the window light doing the work
✓Straight verticals, even if it means a tighter frame
✓A real sky, including a gray one
✓Lamps off unless it is genuinely a twilight shot
✓Generous headroom, because every layout crops from the top
✓Full-resolution TIFFs plus web JPEGs at 2400px
Reject these
✕Replaced skies, especially the purple ones
✕HDR halos around window frames and rooflines
✕16mm interiors that bend the room into a fisheye
✕Virtual staging that is not disclosed in the caption
✕Grass saturated past anything that grows in Florida
✕A logo or watermark burned into the frame
04 · Files and naming

subject  joelyn · jessica · duo · property slug
wardrobe  white · red · black
ground  lightbg · darkbg
crop  headshot · threeqtr · half · full
orientation  vert · horz · square · ultrawide
state  master · web · thumb
This convention already caught one error in this project: a file named joelyn-red turned out to be Jessica in a white jacket. The naming is only useful if the person exporting the files actually checks the frame.
Every agent needs eight frames
Vertical, half-body and seated, on both a light and a dark ground. That is the minimum the card, signage and social layouts require without recropping something into the wrong aspect.
Reshoot every two years
Nothing dates a real estate brand faster than a headshot that no longer looks like the person answering the door.
Get the license in writing
Property photography is usually licensed to the listing, not to the brokerage in perpetuity. Ask for print, web and social in writing, or a sold listing's photos cannot legally appear in a portfolio.
