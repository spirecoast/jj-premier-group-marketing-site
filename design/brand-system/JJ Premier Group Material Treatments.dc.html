Material treatments
The mark exists to be made in something. Brass, etched glass, blind deboss, bookcloth, powder-coated steel. Each one is a different answer to the same question: how does the wordmark hold when it stops being ink?

07 · Rules for anything physical
One process per piece
Foil or deboss or ink, never two together and never all three. A single treatment executed properly always outclasses a stack of them.
Matte, always
Matte foil, matte vinyl, matte sign faces, satin brass. Gloss reads as a franchise print shop under any light, and outdoors it turns into glare.
The seal handles small and round
Under 40mm, or on any curved or woven surface, the two-line lockup gives way to the seal. Always with PREMIER GROUP under the rule.
No gold, ever
Satin brass is a substrate; gold foil is a decoration. Brass is permitted because it is the actual metal. Gold ink and gold foil are not in the system.
Before you order
These are design intents, not press proofs. Every substrate shifts color: brass warms Deep Harbor, navy powder coat drifts green under LED, and Linen on woven cloth prints half a tone darker than on paper. Ask for a physical proof on the real material before any run over 50 units, and check the Coldwell Banker mark placement against current franchise rules on anything permanent.
