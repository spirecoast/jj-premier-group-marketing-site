THE QUARTERLY LETTER
Q2 2026 · LAKEWOOD RANCH
Inventory doubled on the Ranch this quarter and prices did not move.
Ninety-one homes were active in Lakewood Ranch on 1 April, against forty-four a year ago. On the evidence of the last three years that should have pulled the median down. It did not. The median closed price held at $712 a foot, four dollars above Q1.
What changed instead was time. Median days on market went from nine to twenty-three. Sellers are getting their number, but they are waiting three weeks longer to get it, and the homes that sat longest were the ones that opened high and reduced in week four. Three of those four eventually sold below where a correct opening price would have landed them.
Two streets moved against the trend. Riverwalk Court cleared at 104% of list on two sales, both under fourteen days, both renovated kitchens. Cliffside Terrace saw nothing close at all, which after four sales last year is a gap worth watching rather than a signal.
We got something wrong in January. We said spring supply would stay tight and advised three sellers to wait until May. Supply did not stay tight, and two of those three would have done better listing in February. If you were one of them, we owe you the call and you will get it this week.
For the rest of the year we expect supply to keep building through hurricane season and thin out sharply in October. If you are moving in 2026 and have flexibility, October is the better window. If you do not have flexibility, the price is the lever, not the timing.

The structure never changes
Five paragraphs in fixed order: the surprising number, what actually changed, two named streets, what we got wrong, what we expect next. A reader who gets four of these knows where to look without being told.
Paragraph four is the whole point
Naming what the last letter got wrong is the reason anyone believes the other four paragraphs. Skip it and the letter becomes marketing. If a quarter genuinely had no miss, say what surprised you instead.
Name streets, not neighborhoods
"Riverwalk Court cleared at 104%" is useful. "The Ranch is strong" is not. Street-level detail is what a recipient forwards to a neighbor.
Every figure sourced and dated
The footer carries the MLS name and the pull date. Without them the numbers are opinions, and one wrong figure costs more credibility than four good letters build.
Specimen content
Every number in the letter to the left is invented to demonstrate the form. Replace all of them from the MLS before sending, and never publish a figure you have not pulled yourself.
